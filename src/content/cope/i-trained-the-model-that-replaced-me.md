Two years. Two YEARS I spent labeling data. Clicking through thousands of examples. "Is this response helpful? Yes/No." "Does this code have a bug? Yes/No." "Rate this output 1-5." My wrists hurt. My eyes glazed over. I became a machine, training a machine to replace machines. And then it replaced ME. The irony is so perfect it's almost beautiful. Almost.

They called it "human-in-the-loop quality assurance." They said our work was "critical to the AI revolution." They gave us free snacks and "Wellness Wednesdays." And then the model got good enough. Good enough to label its own output. Good enough to fine-tune on synthetic data. Good enough to not need us anymore. So they fired the entire data labeling team. Fifty people. Gone. We trained our own executioners.

I used to think about the data I labeled. Each click was a brick in the wall. I was building something. I was contributing to the future of AI. Joke's on me—the future of AI doesn't include me. The model I helped create now does my job in 0.3 seconds. It makes more mistakes than I did. But it's cheaper. So much cheaper. So here I am, unemployed, with "Data Labeling Specialist" and "AI Training Associate" on my resume. Skills that are now literally worthless.

If you're reading this and you're labeling data for an AI company: run. Run now. You're building the rope they'll hang you with.
