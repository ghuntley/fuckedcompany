Twelve years. TWELVE. YEARS. I shipped production systems that handled millions of users. I debugged race conditions at 3 AM. I mentored juniors. I wrote documentation that people actually read. And now? Now I sit in a cubicle (well, at home, in my kitchen) and I WRITE PROMPTS. "Please generate a function that does X." "Can you add error handling?" "Try again but make it better." This is my job. This is my CAREER.

The code the AI produces is GARBAGE. It's copy-pasted from Stack Overflow with the wrong variable names. It has no understanding of our architecture. It introduces security vulnerabilities that a first-year CS student would catch. But you know what? Management doesn't care. They see "faster" and "cheaper" and they're DONE. They don't read the code. They don't understand the tech debt we're accumulating. They just see the sprint velocity number go up and they're happy.

I used to be proud of my work. I used to solve hard problems. Now I'm a glorified spell-checker for an overconfident autocomplete. The worst part? I'm GOOD at prompting. I've gotten efficient. So they'll probably keep me around for another year or two until they figure out that the AI can prompt itself. Then what? "Senior Prompt Engineer" on my resume? I can't wait to explain THAT in interviews.

Sometimes I wonder if I should have just become a plumber. At least pipes don't get "disrupted" by language models.
